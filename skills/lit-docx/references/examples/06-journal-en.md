# Why schoolyards

Children spend roughly ninety minutes a day outdoors at school during the summer term, most of it on hard surfaces. A playground that reaches 50 °C at lunchtime is too hot to sit on and uncomfortable to run across, and staff in two of the six schools in this study had begun to keep younger classes indoors on hot afternoons.

The district estates plan for 2027 to 2031 sets aside money for outdoor improvements but does not say what kind. This paper gives the estates committee one summer of local measurements to compare the two options most schools have asked for: shade sails and tree planting.

## The six schools

The schools were chosen to cover the main yard types in the district. Two have open asphalt yards with no shade, two have a row of mature trees along one edge, and two installed shade sails between 2022 and 2024. Yard areas range from 1,100 to 2,900 square metres.

# Measurement campaign

Each school received four surface sensors and one air-temperature logger mounted 1.5 metres above the ground in a ventilated screen. Sensors were fixed flush with the surface on unshaded asphalt, on asphalt under the existing shade, on artificial grass where present, and on bare soil or bark beds.

Readings were taken every fifteen minutes from 2 June to 29 August 2026. We report the afternoon peak, defined as the highest reading between 12:00 and 16:00, because that is when lunch play and afternoon sport take place.

## Data quality

Of 24 surface sensors, two failed in July and were replaced within four days. The gaps are left out of the averages rather than filled. Air temperatures agreed with the nearest public weather station to within 0.8 °C on 91 % of days.

# Surface temperature by cover

The differences between surfaces were larger than the differences between schools. On the hottest day of the campaign, 18 July, air temperature at the six schools peaked between 31.4 and 33.0 °C.

Table 1. Afternoon peak surface temperature on 18 July 2026

| Surface | Peak (°C) | Above air (°C) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Asphalt, unshaded | 58 | 25 |
| Artificial grass | 63 | 30 |
| Asphalt under sail | 41 | 8 |
| Asphalt under trees | 36 | 3 |
| Bark bed under trees | 31 | -2 |

Note: Means of the sensors on each surface across the six schools.

Artificial grass ran hotter than asphalt at every school that had it. Shade sails removed most of the excess heat, and mature trees removed almost all of it, because the canopy also cools the air beneath it through evaporation.

Across the whole summer the pattern held. Unshaded asphalt exceeded 45 °C on 31 afternoons; asphalt under sails did so on none. Under trees the highest reading of the summer was 38 °C.

::: callout kind=note title="Reading the temperatures"
A surface temperature is not the same as the air temperature a child feels. It matters for contact: a seat, a ball court or a fall. The district play-safety note treats surfaces above 50 °C as a burn risk for prolonged skin contact.
:::

# Cost per shaded square metre

Sails and trees differ in what they cost now and what they cost later. A sail gives full shade from the day it is installed but needs a new canopy every ten to twelve years. A tree planted at nursery standard size gives little shade for the first five years and full shade from about year fifteen.

Table 2. Cost per shaded square metre over 25 years

| Item | Sails (£/m²) | Trees (£/m²) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Install or plant | 62 | 25 |
| Care, years 1 to 10 | 8 | 31 |
| Replacement canopy | 54 | 0 |
| Care, years 11 to 25 | 12 | 18 |
| Total over 25 years | 136 | 74 |

Source: District estates framework prices, 2026 schedule (sample).

Over the first ten years sails cost £70 per shaded square metre against £56 for trees. That comparison favours trees on paper, but young trees give on average about a quarter of their mature shade in those years, so the cost of shade actually delivered in years one to ten is about three times higher for trees. From year twelve onwards trees are clearly cheaper.

# Recommendation

Most of the six yards can do both. The main play area, where children gather at lunch and where a quick result matters, suits a sail. The yard edges and the space beside classroom walls suit trees, which need room for roots and do not block ball games.

We recommend that the committee fund sails over the main play area at the two unshaded schools in 2027, at an estimated cost of £96,000, and plant 48 trees along the edges of all six yards in the same year for £31,000.

## Limits of the trial

One summer is a short record, and 2026 was warmer than the ten-year average in the district. The study measured surfaces, not children's comfort or activity levels. A second summer of logging at the two schools that receive sails would show how much of the modelled reduction appears in practice.
