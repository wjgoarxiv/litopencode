# Summary

The Larkfield bike-share scheme carried 540,000 trips between January and June 2026, 18 % more than in the same months of 2025. Demand grew fastest on the electric bikes, which make up a quarter of the fleet but carried 41 % of trips.

::: keyfigures
- **540,000** trips, January to June 2026
  - Scheme back office, docked trips over two minutes
- **88.6 %** average fleet availability
  - Monthly mean; target 90 %
- **£0.76** maintenance cost per trip
  - £412,000 spend over 540,000 trips
:::

Availability missed its target in the first four months and recovered in May and June. The workshop was short of e-bike batteries for most of the spring, and the single repair van could not keep up with the rise in brake and tyre faults once trip numbers climbed.

Maintenance spending ran £32,000 over budget, mostly on staff overtime and parts. For the second half of the year the team proposes a second repair van, which costs less than the weekend overtime it would replace, and a stock of forty spare batteries.

# Fleet and usage

The scheme has 1,200 bikes at 140 docking stations: 900 classic bikes and 300 electric bikes. No bikes or stations were added in the period, so all growth came from more trips per bike.

## Trips by month

Table 1. Trips by month, January to June 2026

| Month | Trips | Trips per bike per day | Change on 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| January | 61,000 | 1.64 | +9 % |
| February | 58,400 | 1.74 | +11 % |
| March | 79,200 | 2.13 | +16 % |
| April | 96,500 | 2.68 | +19 % |
| May | 118,300 | 3.18 | +21 % |
| June | 126,600 | 3.52 | +22 % |
| Total | 540,000 | 2.49 | +18 % |

Source: Scheme back office trip export, July 2026 (sample).

Weekday trips peaked between 07:30 and 09:00 and again between 17:00 and 18:30. Weekend trips were spread across the afternoon and were longer, with a median of 24 minutes against 13 minutes on weekdays.

## Electric bikes

The 300 electric bikes carried 221,400 trips, an average of 4.1 trips per bike per day against 2.0 for classic bikes. Riders chose them for the longer routes into the hospital campus and the north estates, where the climb from the river makes a classic bike hard work.

Heavy use has a cost. Each e-bike battery is rated for about 800 full charges, and the busiest bikes reached that point in under two years rather than the three the supplier assumed.

# Availability and repairs

Availability is the share of the fleet that is docked, charged and fit to ride at the 06:00 count. The contract target is 90 %.

## Availability by month

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| January | 89.1 % | 76.7 % | 86.0 % |
| February | 88.4 % | 75.6 % | 85.2 % |
| March | 90.6 % | 80.6 % | 88.1 % |
| April | 91.2 % | 84.0 % | 89.4 % |
| May | 92.2 % | 88.6 % | 91.3 % |
| June | 92.3 % | 89.5 % | 91.6 % |

Note: Whole-fleet figures weight classic and electric bikes 3 to 1.

The electric fleet pulled the average down until a delayed battery order of 60 units arrived in late April. Since then e-bike availability has stayed close to the classic fleet.

## Repair causes

The workshop closed 3,840 repair jobs, up from 3,010 in the first half of 2025. Brakes and tyres together account for half of all jobs and grew in line with trip numbers.

Table 3. Repair jobs by cause, January to June 2026

| Cause | Jobs | Share | Median days off road |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Brakes | 1,020 | 26.6 % | 1 |
| Tyres and tubes | 910 | 23.7 % | 1 |
| Battery or motor | 640 | 16.7 % | 6 |
| Chain and gears | 560 | 14.6 % | 2 |
| Vandalism | 430 | 11.2 % | 4 |
| Dock lock faults | 280 | 7.3 % | 3 |
| Total | 3,840 | 100.0 % | 2 |

Source: Workshop job system (sample).

Battery and motor faults kept bikes off the road longest, six days at the median, because parts came from a single supplier with a four-day delivery time.

# Maintenance cost

Spending reached £412,000 against a half-year budget of £380,000. Most of the overrun was overtime for the two mechanics who covered weekend call-outs in April and May.

Table 4. Maintenance budget and spend, January to June 2026

| Item | Budget (£) | Spend (£) | Difference (£) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Workshop staff | 236,000 | 248,000 | 12,000 |
| Parts | 82,000 | 96,000 | 14,000 |
| Battery replacement | 36,000 | 41,000 | 5,000 |
| Van, fuel and insurance | 26,000 | 27,000 | 1,000 |
| Total | 380,000 | 412,000 | 32,000 |

Source: Council finance ledger, cost centre 4417 (sample).

Cost per trip fell from £0.81 in the first half of 2025 to £0.76, because trips grew faster than spending. The overrun is therefore a budget-setting problem as much as a cost problem: the 2026 budget assumed flat demand.

# Priorities for July to December

The team has three priorities for the second half of the year, set out below with their costs.

## Second repair van

A second van would let one mechanic run brake and tyre rounds while the other handles battery swaps and station faults. Lease and running costs are £14,000 for six months. The team estimates it would remove most weekend overtime, which cost £21,000 between March and June.

## Battery stock

Holding forty spare batteries would let the workshop swap a worn battery the same day instead of waiting for delivery. The stock costs £24,000 and draws down as batteries are replaced, so most of it moves forward spending already planned for 2027.

## Revised 2027 budget

The 2027 maintenance budget will be built from the expected trip count multiplied by the cost per trip, rather than from last year's spend. The finance team will receive the trip forecast with the October budget round.
