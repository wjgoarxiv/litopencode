::: callout kind=key title="Decision requested"
Approve a six-month trial of Sunday opening, 12:00 to 16:00, at Eastfield, Kings Row, Millbrook and Thorne branches from 4 January 2027, at a cost of £19,600 from the libraries modernisation budget.
:::

# Summary

Three of the borough's seven branches already open on Sundays, and they are busy. They averaged 310 visits each Sunday afternoon in the year to September 2026, which is more per opening hour than any weekday evening.

Families and shift workers are the main Sunday users. In the 2026 user survey, 44 % of Sunday visitors said they could not come on any other day, against 19 % of weekday visitors.

The trial costs £19,600 for twenty-six Sundays. Almost all of it is staff time: two members of staff per branch for four hours, paid at the weekend rate.

We will judge the trial on visits per opening hour. If the four branches reach two thirds of the current Sunday average by the end of June, we will propose permanent Sunday opening in the 2027/28 budget.

# Current Sunday use

Table 1. Visits per Sunday afternoon and per opening hour (weekday: evening hours) at the three Sunday branches, October 2025 to September 2026

| Branch | Sunday visits | Per hour, Sunday | Per hour, weekday |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Central | 420 | 105 | 71 |
| Harbour Road | 280 | 70 | 48 |
| Westgate | 230 | 58 | 39 |
| Average | 310 | 78 | 53 |

Source: Door counter data, library management system (sample).

The four trial branches were chosen because they are the furthest from an existing Sunday branch. Residents of Eastfield and Thorne currently need two buses to reach Central on a Sunday.

# Cost and staffing

Table 2. Trial cost for twenty-six Sundays

| Item | Basis | Cost (£) |
|---|---|---:|
| Staff time | 2 staff × 4 h × 26 Sundays × 4 branches × £18.50 | 15,392 |
| Heating, lighting and security | £40 per branch per Sunday | 4,160 |
| Total | Rounded to £19,600 in the request | 19,552 |

Staff will work Sundays by volunteer rota first. The union branch has agreed the rota terms, and fourteen staff have already put their names forward, enough to cover all four branches without compulsory shifts.

# Risks

Turnout may be slow in the first weeks while residents learn about the change. Leaflets go to every household in the four catchment areas in December, and each branch will promote Sunday opening at its weekday story sessions.

A wet winter would depress visits at all branches. To keep the comparison fair, results will be read against the three existing Sunday branches over the same weeks, not against last year.

# Next steps

With approval by 31 October, the rota closes on 21 November and the four branches publish their Sunday hours in the December newsletter. A mid-trial report on visits per opening hour goes to the libraries board in April 2027, and the final report with the budget proposal in July.
