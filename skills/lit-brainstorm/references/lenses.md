# Idea lenses

A lens is a way of looking at the question that makes some options visible and hides others. A pool built through one lens is one idea in different clothes. Use at least four lenses per session, name the lens beside each option, and add one that changes the premise before the pool closes.

Every lens below has the same three parts: the prompt to ask of the question, what it tends to surface, and when it is wasted effort.

## The core lenses

### 1. The user

- **Prompt:** who is this for, what are they doing the moment before and the moment after, and what would make them say "that is exactly it"?
- **Surfaces:** options shaped by a real person's day rather than by the builder's tools.
- **Wasted when:** the audience is the builder alone and has already said what they want.

### 2. The constraint

- **Prompt:** what is the tightest limit here (time, money, size, a dependency, a rule), and what is the best option that lives inside it? Then: which single limit, if removed, would change the answer most?
- **Surfaces:** cheap options that are better than the expensive default, and the hidden cost of a limit nobody questioned.
- **Wasted when:** the constraints are invented rather than given. Never treat a guess as a limit.

### 3. The analogy

- **Prompt:** where has a similar question been answered well in another field, product, craft or era, and what did they do?
- **Surfaces:** structures and metaphors the builder's own field does not offer.
- **Wasted when:** the analogy is chosen for charm and does not transfer. State what carries over and what does not.

### 4. The inversion

- **Prompt:** what would make this worse on purpose, and what is the opposite of each of those? Or: what if the goal were the reverse?
- **Surfaces:** failure modes turned into requirements, and options that exist only because the obvious one was refused.
- **Wasted when:** the question is a pure naming or taste question with no failure to invert.

### 5. The removal

- **Prompt:** what if the thing were half the size, had no screen, no account, no setup, no maintenance? What does the smallest version that still counts look like?
- **Surfaces:** minimum-first options and the part of the ask that is doing no work.
- **Wasted when:** the user asked for the full thing and a smaller version would be a scope cut. Present it as an option, never as the answer.

### 6. The remix

- **Prompt:** which two existing options, ideas or tools can be combined, and what does the combination do that neither does alone?
- **Surfaces:** hybrids. Do this late, after the pool exists, or it collapses variety.
- **Wasted when:** it runs first. Early combination removes the parents from the pool.

### 7. The extreme

- **Prompt:** what is the most ambitious version, and what is the most boring version? What does each teach about the middle?
- **Surfaces:** the range of the space, and a sense of which dimension the user actually cares about.
- **Wasted when:** the ambitious version is silently treated as a recommendation.

### 8. The sequence

- **Prompt:** what could be done first, second, third? Which order lets the user learn the most before committing?
- **Surfaces:** staged approaches and cheap experiments.
- **Wasted when:** it turns the brainstorm into a plan. Stay at the level of "which first step", and leave the checklist to `lit-plan`.

### 9. The outsider

- **Prompt:** what would a newcomer to this field do, who has never been told the usual way? What would an expert in a distant field do?
- **Surfaces:** options that skip conventions the builder no longer notices.
- **Wasted when:** the convention is a hard requirement. Name the requirement so it is not violated by accident.

### 10. The existing material

- **Prompt:** what already exists in this project, folder or toolbox that does most of the job? What is one small change away from useful?
- **Surfaces:** reuse options, and the observation that the plain default is already half built.
- **Wasted when:** the question is a blank-page one with no project behind it.

## The naming set

Names are the one question where raw quantity matters most and where one lens produces a monoculture. Run these five and mix them:

| Lens | Prompt | Example shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sound | What reads well aloud, in a single breath, in the user's language and in English? | Two short syllables, a hard consonant, a vowel that closes. |
| Meaning | What does the product do for the person, in a single word or image? | A verb, a place where the thing happens, an outcome. |
| Metaphor | What well-known thing behaves like this product? | A tool, an animal, a craft, a landscape feature. |
| Compound | Which two plain words, joined, say something neither says alone? | Word plus word, prefix plus root, a clipped phrase. |
| Plain | What would a descriptive name be, with no cleverness at all? | Verb plus object. Always include it; it is the baseline every clever name must beat. |

For names, add the checks the user will otherwise do later and say they were not run: spelling and pronunciation in the user's languages, accidental meanings, and any obvious clash with a name the user already knows about. Flag them as unchecked; this surface does not search registries, trademarks or domains and never implies clearance.

## Choosing and mixing

- Ordinary open question: user, constraint, analogy, inversion, existing material.
- Product or feature direction: user, constraint, removal, extreme, sequence.
- Naming: the naming set plus one premise-changing lens such as inversion.
- Stuck pool: outsider, then extreme, then remix over whatever exists.
- Before closing the pool, add one lens whose premise differs from every lens used so far.

## What makes a good raw option

- One or two lines, concrete enough that two people would picture the same thing.
- A different kind of answer from its neighbours, not a rewording.
- Marked as an assumption if it depends on a fact nobody has checked.
- Carries its lens in a short tag, such as `[inversion]`, so variety can be audited at a glance.

## Failure signals

- Every option starts with the same verb or the same noun.
- No option would make the user say "I had not thought of that".
- The plain default appears nowhere in the pool, so nothing is compared with it.
- All options came from the lens you like best.
- The pool grew by splitting one option into three flavours.

When a signal fires, stop generating through the current lens, pick a lens that has not been used, and write only options that the earlier ones ruled out.
